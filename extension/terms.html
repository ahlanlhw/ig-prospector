Last updated: 28 July 2026 · Operated by Willow Tech

What Sprill is: a business-development workspace that helps businesses filter the right public Instagram accounts and run meaningful, personalized outreach, with a built-in CRM tracker — easy to set up, easy to export.

What it is not: it is not a bulk-DM bot. It does not blast identical messages, and sending never runs unattended — every message goes to one person at a time, from a template you wrote, capped at 5 direct messages and 10 comments a day and paced with a minimum interval. There is no batch comment mode at all.

Capture is different from sending. If you start a recording session, Sprill collects handles from the Instagram pages you browse, on its own, until the timer you set runs out. Read section 3 so you know exactly what the tool does in your browser.

2. How sending and commenting work

Sprill writes to two Instagram surfaces: direct messages, and comments on a post you have open. Which mode is active is your choice, and it changes how much the software does on your behalf:

  • Insert only (the default, for both DMs and comments). Sprill writes the text into Instagram's message composer or comment box. You press Send or Post.
  • One-click send (opt-in, off until you turn it on). Sprill opens a temporary browser tab on the recipient's profile, navigates to the direct-message thread, and clicks Instagram's Send button for you. You trigger each send from the panel; the click itself is performed by the software.
  • Batch send (requires one-click). The same sequence, repeated over the handles you ticked, one at a time, with a pause between each and subject to the same daily and monthly caps.
  • Auto-post comments (opt-in, separate from one-click send, off until you turn it on). Sprill types your text into the comment box on the post you are viewing and clicks Post for you. The comment is public and attributed to your account, visible to anyone who can see the post. You trigger each one; the click itself is performed by the software. Comments have their own daily cap and their own pacing, separate from direct messages, and there is no batch comment mode.

You are solely responsible for the content you send and for the outcomes, including any action Instagram may take on your account.

3. Automated activity you are authorizing

So that there is no ambiguity about what the software does in your browser, these are all of the actions Sprill can take without a separate confirmation for each one:

  • Opening and closing background tabs on public Instagram profiles, to read a bio and score a lead. This happens automatically after a page scan, up to the "auto-enrich" number you set, and after a recording session.
  • Opening a background tab on a message thread to check whether a message you sent has been read.
  • Opening a tab and clicking Send, if you have enabled one-click or batch send.
  • Clicking Post on a comment, if you have enabled auto-post comments. This publishes text under your account where anyone who can see the post can read it.
  • Reading the public engagement numbers already displayed on a profile's post grid — view, like and comment counts, and post dates — so it can compare an account's posts against that same account's median and point out the ones that overperformed. No post is opened and no post content is stored. It never tells you why a post did well; that is for you to judge.
  • Reading handles visible on Instagram pages you browse, for the duration of a recording session you started.

All of it happens locally, in your own browser, under your own logged-in Instagram session. Sprill has no server-side access to your Instagram account and cannot act on it while your browser is closed. Caps and pacing are defaults enforced in the extension on your device, not guarantees.
